Tensions discovered across the course

Where the picture gets more complicated

Contradiction
More documentation ↔ less time
Providers are being asked to prove more while also needing technology to give clinicians time back.
Market tension
All-in-one ↔ best-of-breed
Customers want things to feel simple, but specialized tools can still do some individual jobs better.
Technology tension
Interoperability can help the suite or make switching easier
Better connections can make a larger platform more useful, but they can also make it easier to add or switch to specialized tools.
Adoption tension
More features ↔ more complexity
Adding more can make the platform stronger, but it can also make it harder for users to find what they already have.
Signal outside the usual ABA software market

Asia may be showing a broader version of what autism technology can look like.

HKUST’s AI4ASD is not a direct ABA software competitor. That is what makes it interesting as a weak signal.

U.S. ABA software lens

  • Run the ABA organization
  • Scheduling + billing + authorizations
  • Clinical documentation + data collection
  • Operational reporting

AI4ASD signal

  • At-home screening
  • One long-term child profile
  • Multiple intervention schools, including ABA
  • Caregiver + clinician continuity
  • Screen → refer → treat → follow
Strategic question: If autism technology starts covering more of the care journey, what should CentralReach handle itself, what should it connect to, and what should it leave to partners?
Why this signal matters
It widens the competitive frame. Instead of only watching ABA practice-management vendors, the project also needs to watch technologies that connect screening, long-term records, caregivers, and multiple care settings.
What not to assume
This does not mean CentralReach should copy AI4ASD or rebuild itself around screening. The value of the signal is that it shows a different way the category could evolve.
